## Decision in brief

**Build B has a practical first implementation:** the existing three.js renderer plus a lazy BVH capsule controller, existing stairs/openings, independently moving door colliders, and a small licensed character set. The difficult parts are stair clearance, moving-door collisions, camera occlusion and measured performance. The research below separates inspected package sizes from future production-bundle and device measurements.

**Build A is feasible as a sourced estimate and drawn-boundary workflow, with material scope decisions.** A no-card service is not necessarily compatible with the licence allowlist, and an open tile URL is not permission to redistribute satellite imagery. No source verified this run supplies an open cadastral boundary and survey-grade plot terrain for Mauritius. Do not promise automatic legal boundaries, reliable earthwork volumes from 30 m terrain, or an approval from generic planning tables. The tables identify the usable components, licence conflicts and evidence still needed.

The preferred no-card cartographic route is MapLibre with OpenFreeMap **if Victor permits its required CC BY/OFL components**. Under the literal current allowlist, that is not yet a shipping decision. A bounded original map made from permitted OSM feature data can support a reduced local drawing workflow, but is not a replacement for a worldwide tiled/satellite basemap. Do not silently make that product substitution. Google is a later, separately authorized provider; Stage 1 creates no key or billing account.

The persistent report banner will be exactly:

> Estimates from public data for planning a conversation, not a survey and not an approval.

Each result will distinguish a user-drawn boundary, public-data estimate, survey measurement, user assumption and unavailable value. A missing value is never converted to zero or presented as a successful query with no features.

## Build A — provider comparison

### Decisions and material gaps

- Prefer MapLibre GL JS for the lazy map renderer and a replaceable provider interface. MapLibre is BSD-3-Clause; **OpenFreeMap is operationally free but the full supplied stack is not wholly within the user's licence allowlist**. OpenMapTiles schema cartographic design and default styles use CC BY 4.0; Noto fonts use OFL. An original style alone is not proven to remove the schema design licence. Do not silently ship the standard Liberty style as “all MIT”.
- No plot-scale satellite layer satisfying **all** of no account/no card, commercial application use, and the strict CC0/MIT/Apache/BSD/ODbL asset allowlist was verified. Esri's reachable anonymous World Imagery URL is not evidence of a commercial licence. NASA GIBS offers lawful no-key imagery under its data policy, but coarse MODIS imagery cannot identify a 500 m² plot, and public-domain/data-policy terms need explicit acceptance under this whitelist. Ask Victor whether to relax those specific licence categories; otherwise state satellite unavailable in the initial free build.
- No publicly documented, openly licensed Mauritius cadastral API/WMS was found. LAVIMS is real, but its existence does not establish public retrieval or redistribution rights. Use a **user-drawn indicative boundary**, never label OSM landuse or a detected building footprint as a cadastral parcel.
- Prefer point-specific PVGIS 5.3 through the existing server catch-all. The API explicitly forbids AJAX; it is not a browser CORS workaround problem. Keep the existing baked point for existing no-site estimates and add an opt-in site calculation.
- Public terrain is regional context, not survey quality. A 500 m² square is only 22.36 m across, smaller than one 30 m DEM cell. Show indicative regional slope/height only; cut/fill and foundation ground levels require survey points/contours with stated vertical datum and accuracy.
- Photon is the best no-key prototype search candidate. Trigger on Search, cache, throttle and permit fallback to pan/coordinate input. Nominatim's current policy needs prominent acknowledgement and a deliberate developer decision, with at most 1 request/second across the whole app and no autocomplete.
- Do not use a shared free OpenTopography key in the product: its terms forbid keys embedded to serve third parties and require Enterprise keys for commercial software. Open-Elevation's current free service is **250 m**, not 30 m, and its allowance counts coordinates.
- Google Open Buildings V3 explicitly lists Mauritius and offers ODbL. Microsoft's current global release is **CDLA Permissive 2.0**, not the historical ODbL licence; exclude under the present allowlist.

### Google example, with assumptions explicit

One deliberate search session = one Dynamic Maps load (including a satellite map type) + one Geocoding request, no Places autocomplete, no Street View, no Elevation call. Once each SKU's free allowance is exhausted but usage stays in the first paid band: **$0.007 + $0.005 = $0.012 per session**. At 1,000 such monthly sessions and no other shared billing-account use: $0. At 20,000: (20,000−10,000)×($7+$5)/1,000 = **$120/month** before applicable taxes/currency conversion. Mauritius does not create a special “Mauritius plot” SKU. Account/billing eligibility, paid overages and terms remain Victor's decision. The old recurring $200 credit ended February 2025; do not budget using it. Sources A11–A14.

If Places autocomplete is selected later its billing/session rules must be costed separately. Restrict the key, configure quota caps; a budget alert is not a hard spending stop. Do not create a Google provider/environment variable in Stage 1.

### Plot accuracy and amount implications

Terrarium values encode `(R*256 + G + B/256) − 32768` metres. The 1/256 m encoding step is **precision, not measurement accuracy**. Upsampling a ~30 m source into a high-zoom texture does not reveal survey-scale ground detail. Terrain source docs explicitly describe oversampling and possible large-building/coastal artifacts. Copernicus is a DSM including trees/buildings. A 22.36 m-wide plot can lie inside one native cell; four interpolated corner values therefore do not provide four independent surveyed heights.

For cut/fill, require an imported survey with breaklines/spot heights and a declared vertical reference, or show a user-entered hypothetical plane as a **scenario**. Suggested evidence target is a suitably surveyed ~0.5–1 m ground grid or well-distributed spot heights with centimetre/decimetre vertical quality appropriate to the site; this is an engineering acceptance proposal, not a guarantee any free source supplies it. Vertical error of 0.20 m uniformly over 500 m² corresponds to 100 m³ volume uncertainty (area × height), demonstrating why regional DEM-based “exact” earthworks would be misleading. A 30 m DEM may contextualize regional fall; it must not silently alter existing foundation quantities.

Sun yield is not a parcel shading survey. Query PVcalc for `peakpower=1`, declared system loss and radiation dataset, with optimization explicitly requested when reporting optimal tilt/orientation. Preserve the PVGIS metadata, yearly `E_y` and calculated optimum. A true solar-path visualization requires a separate astronomical model; PVGIS energy output alone does not encode building-by-building shadows. Site report line: **“Estimates from public data for planning a conversation, not a survey and not an approval.”**

### Formula and units decisions

Store area in square metres. Display **perches≈area/42.21** and **arpents≈area/4221**, labelled “Mauritius display conversion:1perche≈42.21m²;1arpent=100perches≈4221m²” [P13–P15]. Do not silently interchange the English perch or acre. This is a documented rounded convention; survey/deed measurements govern.

For sourced setback rules, use a robust polygon offset/erosion operation that respects the actual polygon, preserves disconnected components, supports the applicable edge-specific setbacks, and subtracts verified reserves. Intersecting all inward half-planes is invalid for concave polygons because it can collapse the result to the polygon kernel. Reject unsupported or invalid shapes explicitly. Do not subtract setback width times perimeter as an area shortcut. Corner-specific setbacks, narrow necks, concave polygons and complete erosion need separate tested geometry.

Planning envelope footprint ceiling = min(usable setback polygon area, applicable coverage fraction × applicable plot area), only after confirming both definitions. FAR, if sourced, independently limits total counted floor area to FAR×its specified plot-area denominator. Height and storey limits independently constrain the model; do not multiply a footprint by an arbitrary floor count and call it permission. Parking, access, drainage, wastewater, easements and other constraints can reduce practical capacity further. Keep “geometric envelope under selected assumptions” separate from “approved buildable area”.

## Metres, area, slope and earthwork — proposed calculation contract

**Pick:** an isolated, tested WGS84 ECEF → local East/North/Up implementation for plot-scale geometry, using published mathematical formulas rather than copying library code. Keep the conversion in the lazy site module. It needs no API key, quota, attribution asset or runtime network request. Use a known geographic origin near the plot centre and an explicit design translation/rotation. This works in Mauritius, the UK and elsewhere, with dateline/pole handling tested; reject unsupported very large extents rather than treating a district as a flat plot.

WGS84 uses semi-major axis `a = 6378137 m`, flattening `f = 1/298.257223563`, eccentricity squared `e²=f(2−f)`. For radians latitude φ, longitude λ and a common reference ellipsoid height h, `N=a/sqrt(1−e²sin²φ)`, and ECEF is `((N+h)cosφ cosλ, (N+h)cosφ sinλ, (N(1−e²)+h)sinφ)`. Subtract the origin ECEF and rotate into the origin's East/North/Up basis. Store longitude/latitude order explicitly; use degrees only at the boundary of this module. Keep orthometric terrain elevations and their vertical datum separate from ellipsoidal horizontal-reference height.

For an initial north-up plan, design `x = originX + E`, `y = originY − N`; if the existing design has another north bearing, apply its explicit rotation. Rooms are neither translated nor rotated by importing a site. Let the user align the boundary relative to the existing plan, store that transform and show the true-north arrow. In three, plan y becomes world z; elevation is world y.

**Error for a 50 m plot:** mathematical projection error is not boundary accuracy. For distance at most 50 m from the local origin, a spherical sanity bound with R≈6,371,000 m gives projection length difference `d³/(6R²)≈5.13×10⁻¹⁰ m` and curvature sag `d²/(2R)≈0.000196 m`. These are analytical approximations, not a tested accuracy claim. The proposed implementation tolerance is 1 mm against independent ellipsoidal reference fixtures at this scale. A 500 m reference-height mismatch alone changes a 50 m scale by about `50×500/R=0.00392 m`; imagery alignment, boundary taps, source datum and DEM errors are much larger. Display realistic precision and preserve uncertainty.

Mauritius main island (longitude about 57–58° E, south of the equator) falls in **UTM zone 40S**, WGS84 EPSG:32740. Rodrigues is in another zone, so do not infer zone 40 from country alone. UTM is a valid export/interchange choice using proj4js MIT, but raw grid metres include projection scale and grid north differs from true north. At approximately −20.33°, 57.37°, the second-order UTM scale estimate is 0.9996183, about **19 mm short per 50 m** if treated uncorrected as ground distance. Record the CRS, scale and meridian convergence; local ENU avoids that mistaken scale/north assumption. Web-Mercator screen coordinates must never be used as ground metres.

**Area and edges:** after rejecting self-intersections, duplicate vertices, nonfinite coordinates and negligible area, use the local shoelace formula `A = 0.5 |Σ(xᵢyᵢ₊₁ − xᵢ₊₁yᵢ)|`; subtract hole areas if holes are later supported. Perimeter is `Σ hypot(Δx,Δy)`; report longest/shortest user-authored side, not triangle edges. Keep metres/m² as authoritative; Mauritius secondary-unit conversions and their rounding are specified in the planning research. Turf's MIT `area` is a possible independent cross-check, but its spherical method must not silently replace the ellipsoidal/local measurement contract. A whole Turf bundle is unnecessary.

**Slope/aspect:** fit a plane `z=aE+bN+c` to valid elevations in the East/North metre frame and a common vertical datum. Report slope `% = 100 sqrt(a²+b²)` and downslope compass bearing from East/North vector `(−a,−b)`, using `atan2(E,N)` modulo 360°. If fitting in design axes instead, transform the gradient back through the saved plan rotation and y-axis inversion before computing the bearing. Test both north-up and rotated plans. State that this is the best-fit plot plane, with sample count/resolution and residuals, not a claim about every local patch. Flat terrain has no meaningful aspect; insufficient/noisy/coincident data yields “not available”. Corner heights are interpolated public values unless measured. A 500 m² square is only **22.36 m wide**; one 30×30 m cell is **900 m²**. Upsampling such a cell cannot reveal plot grading or retaining-wall levels.

**Cut and fill:** require the platform polygon, chosen level H, a survey/user terrain model, units, vertical datum and confidence. On a piecewise-linear triangulated surface clipped to the platform, integrate `cut = ∫ max(z−H,0)dA`, `fill = ∫ max(H−z,0)dA`. Split triangles where `z=H`; each all-one-sign subtriangle integrates exactly as `area × mean(vertex depths)`. Do not average positive and negative corner depths before separating cut/fill. Report gross cut/fill separately, with no assumed bulking, shrinkage, topsoil or haul factor. Never add platform and foundation excavation blindly: overlapping regions must be subtracted or reported separately as non-additive estimates.

With only regional 30 m terrain, show contextual terrain and withhold actionable plot earthwork quantities. A labelled “illustrative scenario” may be computed from explicit user-entered levels after Victor's choice; it is not measured ground. For credible plot quantities, obtain a licensed topographic survey with breaklines/spot levels, common benchmark and stated horizontal/vertical accuracy, or suitable licensed high-resolution bare-earth LiDAR locally validated against survey. Finer pixel spacing alone does not prove accuracy or bare earth.
