## Dependency order

1. **Keep one measured project.** Add an optional, versioned `Property.city` with site extent, roads, utility routes, infrastructure assets and data-centre assumptions. Metres are canonical. Existing house rooms, floors, foundations, paint, garden, solar and services remain intact. City-wide corridors are separate from the building's internal pipework. A building envelope is a refreshed snapshot of room bounds and storey height, excluding roof projections. It is not a live two-way city/building transform; editing interiors returns to the existing tools.
2. **Draw before estimating.** The same road polygons, routes and asset footprints feed both 2D and 3D. Roads use editable access/cycle/path planning sections. Intersections are unioned, not counted twice. Conflicting layer profiles or unresolved overlaps withhold the affected net total and request review. Utility lengths include elevation differences; shared trench ambiguity is reported. A planning width, pipe diameter or cable route is not authority approval or hydraulic/electrical sizing.
3. **Separate capacity from energy.** Data-centre racks × kW/rack × utilisation determine modelled IT load. PUE adds facility overhead. Annual hours determine energy; PV yield, battery usable capacity and optional WUE remain explicit scenario inputs. Renewable production never reduces the PUE numerator or proves hourly autonomy. Display missing assumptions and avoid claims of carbon neutrality without evidence.
4. **Attach verified supplier references.** Research primary Mauritius supplier pages for road materials, solar street lighting, PV/storage, water/treatment, electrical distribution, EV charging, fibre, racks/cooling and ecology. Exact published data may be linked; families remain quote-only. Generic asset dimensions stay labelled planning envelopes. Product source references neither imply a partnership nor place an order.
5. **Keep procurement private and truthful.** User confirmed separate recipient email choices. Build packages with supplier, quantities, order date, invoice date, delivery date and order/invoice recipients. Use Mauritius civil dates and allow proforma invoice dates before order dates. Keep schedules out of the existing unauthenticated design JSON routes. Separate authenticated merchant-owned storage uses revisions and idempotency. Existing real checkout/fulfilment hooks remain available for onboarded products; a draft schedule is never represented as an issued invoice, sent email or placed order. No invoice or automatic supplier-dispatch provider exists yet, so display that capability honestly rather than invent one.
6. **Organise around decisions.** A dedicated City Build workspace offers Plan/3D, Infrastructure, Data centre, Orders and Overview. Keep map editing and the selected-item inspector together; show only contextual controls, Escape/Back exits, and preserve camera position on selection. Phone panels scroll within the workspace, never cover an inescapable canvas. Existing building tools remain one action away.
7. **Verify before handing over.** Test geometry/overlap/units, state save and history, energy equations, invalid inputs, schedule dates, private-store boundaries and tenant isolation. Run client/API typechecks, build and focused regressions. Use headless Playwright at 1280px desktop and 390px phone, save actual PNGs in repo docs, inspect layouts and record console errors. Keep one Obsidian handoff folder with a price-free pitch summary and screenshots. No migration, message, supplier order or deployment is executed during implementation.

## User journey

Set site dimensions → draw roads and utility corridors → place infrastructure and building envelopes → open the detailed building tools where needed → configure data-centre demand and renewable scenarios → review measured quantities and unresolved engineering choices → attach source-backed supplier references → prepare dated procurement packages and email recipients → save a private merchant draft or export it for review → issue orders/invoices only through an explicitly configured and authorised operational workflow.

## Engineering review boundaries

Road geometry needs swept-path, pavement, drainage and authority review. Water/sewer require capacity, invert, pressure and connection approval. Electrical/fibre corridors require utility coordination and separation/duct design; displayed paths do not size conductors or circuits. Data-centre availability tiers, fire protection, thermal performance and structural capacity need project-specific engineering. The app should support measured choices and transparent quantities without labelling an unchecked template safe or compliant.

## Implementation checkpoint and continuation

The current implementation is a bounded site/district workspace, not a complete certified-city CAD/GIS system. The shared model caps site dimensions at 10 km, each road/network class at 128 segments, total assets at 300, and building/data-centre envelopes at 64. Dense exact geometry can still take about 1.7 seconds on this development host; larger projects need spatial indexing, worker calculation and partitioned loading before raising limits.
